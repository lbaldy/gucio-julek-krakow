# Król Krak (`krak`)

## Role
The king of the settlement under Wawel. Kind, worried for his people, not a warrior.
**Arc:** he trusts swords and knights → his knights run → he doubts the shoemaker ("Szewczyk? Hm…
zobaczymy.") → he sees the sewn sheep and lends his own cart ("Szewcze… może to się uda.") →
he honours Dratewka as wiser than all his knights. The town is named after him.

Scenes: `prologue` card 4 (sighing), `town` (on the bench), `workshop` (the assembly beat),
`outro` cards 2–3.

## Personality
Gentle, grandfatherly, a little helpless and honest about it. Sighs a lot at first. Never
pompous; laughs at himself in the end.

## Look (for art)
A kind old king, ≈ 1.4× Gucio's height, round picture-book proportions. A long grey beard,
rosy cheeks, bushy eyebrows. A simple golden crown **slightly too big** (it slips down on his
forehead until the outro, where it sits straight). A long red tunic with a folk-embroidered
border (cross-stitch flowers), a short fur-trimmed blue cloak, soft leather boots. No sword.

Poses: `idle` (sitting on the bench, sad), `talk`, `stand_hopeful` (hands clasped, in the
workshop doorway), `proud` (arms open, outro). The ref is a standing full-body sheet.

## Voice brief
A warm, gentle older Polish man (60s–70s), soft and grandfatherly, a little tired and
worried, never booming or theatrical. Natural standard Polish. Library audition first.

## Speech style
Short, plain sentences, often with a sigh. Calls the boys "dzieci", Julek "maluchu",
Dratewka "szewcze" then "szewczyku".

### Sample lines
- "Moi rycerze uciekli. Miecze nie pomogą…"
- "Szewcze… może to się uda. Weźcie mój wóz."
- "Szewczyku, byłeś mądrzejszy niż wszyscy moi rycerze."
