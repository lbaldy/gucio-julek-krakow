# Game Design Brief: "Gucio i Julek Odkrywają Polskę – Tajemnica Smoka Wawelskiego"

> **Approved by the user (2026-11-22).** Drafted by Claude from the user's direction.

## Overview

- **Series:** Gucio i Julek Odkrywają Polskę. **Each game stands alone** and can be played in any order: nothing in the story refers to the other games. It reuses the series' voices, the reference art for the boys, and their home.
- **Genre:** Point-and-Click Adventure
- **Language:** Polish only (all voice-over and any on-screen text).
- **Target Audience:** Children (approx. 6 years old). The game must be fully playable by a child who cannot read yet.
- **Length:** about 20–30 minutes.
- **Season:** summer. Green meadows, a warm river, long golden evenings.
- **Theme:** In a dream, Gucio and Julek land in King Krak's settlement under Wawel hill. A greedy dragon is stealing the town's sheep, including a little lamb called Kłębuszek. The boys help the shoemaker Dratewka, whom nobody believes, trick the dragon with a fake sheep. They bring the sheep home, and that's how Kraków got its name.

## Story Depth

The Gniezno game felt thin in its cutscenes. They were mostly narration over still pictures, nobody really wanted anything, and the ending turned into a list of facts. This game fixes that with five rules:

1. **Someone we care about is in trouble.** The dragon doesn't take "a sheep". He takes **Kłębuszek**, the old shepherd's favourite little lamb, and we see it happen in the prologue. The boys promise to bring him back. That one promise drives the whole game, and the ending pays it off.
2. **The sheep are alive.** The greedy dragon doesn't eat the sheep. He hoards them in his cave like treasure, "na później" ("for later"), and counts them before sleep. This makes the stakes real for a 6-year-old (we have to get them out!) without anything sad, and it gives the ending a reunion.
3. **Every main character changes.**
   - **Dratewka** starts out laughed at ("Szewczyk? Na smoka? Ha ha!" — "A shoemaker? Against a dragon? Ha ha!"). He ends as the hero, and he says he couldn't have done it alone.
   - **Krak** first trusts swords and knights, and learns that a clever idea wins.
   - **The shepherd** goes from sad and counting to hugging Kłębuszek.
   - **Julek**'s "Beee!" goes from a silly game in the bedroom to the trick that saves the day.
4. **Show, don't tell.** Characters act and talk, and the narrator only frames the scene, with at most one line per picture. We see the knights run away instead of hearing that they did. Gucio and Julek react in every cutscene: they're in the story, not watching it.
5. **The legend touches real life.** The bedtime frame (Mama reads the legend) and the morning (the boys tell Mama the ending, and she promises a trip to see the real dragon) connect the dream to the real Kraków.

## Main Characters & Mechanics

**Gucio (6 years old):**

- Interacts with objects, collects items and talks to characters.
- Solves logical puzzles and reaches higher places.

**Julek (2 years old):**

- Crawls into small spaces to retrieve objects.
- Scares off or distracts animals with funny noises.
- Uses his toddler charm and unconventional ideas to solve puzzles (often by accident).

Each puzzle should use a *different* Julek trick, so he never does the same thing twice in a row.

**Mama:** reads the legend at bedtime, and hears how it ended in the morning. She's the series' existing character and voice.

**King Krak:** a kind, grey-bearded king with a slightly too big crown. He's worried about his people and isn't a warrior himself. He believes in knights, until his knights run away.

**The old shepherd (stary pasterz):** a kind, slow-talking grandpa with a crook and a straw hat. He counts his sheep every evening, and every morning there's one fewer. His favourite is the little lamb **Kłębuszek** ("Little Ball of Wool"). Wanda isn't in this game: a princess wouldn't keep sheep, and she has her own legend.

**Szewczyk Dratewka:** a young shoemaker. He's kind, a bit clumsy, and very clever. Nobody takes him seriously because he's "just a shoemaker". He has the plan but needs helpers.

**The Wawel Dragon (Smok Wawelski):** big, green and greedy, more silly than scary. He hoards sheep in his cave like treasure and counts them before sleep ("Jedna, dwie… moje!" — "One, two… mine!"). He snores, and puffs of smoke come out of his nostrils. He never eats anything except the fake sheep.

## Plot & Gameplay Structure

### Act I: Bedtime and the Dream (bedroom + dream prologue)

- **Bedtime frame:** a warm **summer** evening, with the window open and crickets outside. The boys play before bed, and Julek bounces on the bed going "Beee! Beee!" (he's a sheep). This sets up the climax.
- Mama sits down with the book about Poland and starts reading the legend: "Dawno, dawno temu, pod wzgórzem Wawel…" ("Long, long ago, under Wawel hill…"). Julek points at the dragon picture: "Smok!"
- Mama stops at the cliffhanger: "…i nikt nie wiedział, jak pokonać smoka. Dokończymy jutro." ("…and nobody knew how to beat the dragon. We'll finish tomorrow.") Gucio protests, but Mama turns off the light. Gucio, sleepily: "Ja bym coś wymyślił…" ("I'd think of something…")
- The book glows on the bedside table, and a little puff of smoke curls out of the dragon page.
- **Dream prologue (voiced picture cards, about 60–75 s):** Mama's voice fades into the narrator's, so the boys fall asleep *into* the story.
  1. A moonlit town under a hill by the river. The shepherd counts his sheep into the fold: "…i Kłębuszek. Wszystkie. Dobranoc, owieczki." ("…and Kłębuszek. All of them. Goodnight, little sheep.")
  2. Two glowing eyes open in the cave. Sniff, sniff. A big green tail slides out.
  3. Morning. The shepherd stands at an open gate. "Kłębuszek?…" Only a tuft of wool is left on the fence.
  4. King Krak's knights march to the cave, a big puff of smoke rolls out, and they run away with their helmets flying (a funny beat). Krak sighs.
  5. The glow takes us to the boys: they wake on the bank of the Vistula in their pyjamas, below Wawel hill. Gucio: "To… to jest ta bajka od mamy!" ("This… this is Mama's story!") Julek: "Smok?" (worried, holding Gucio's hand).

### Act II: The Settlement under Wawel (meeting Krak and Dratewka)

- **Arrival beat (in-world, playable right after):** the knights run past the boys, clanking, and one drops his helmet. Townspeople are hiding and doors are shut.
- King Krak sits sadly on a bench. "Moi rycerze uciekli. Miecze nie pomogą…" ("My knights ran away. Swords won't help…")
- The old shepherd tells them about Kłębuszek and shows them the tuft of wool. **Gucio promises to bring him back.** (This promise is the story's heart, so make it a real moment: a close-up line, music softens.)
- They find Dratewka in his workshop while townspeople laugh at him through the window ("Szewczyk na smoka? Ha ha!"). Gucio believes him. Dratewka's plan: "Zrobimy owcę, która nie jest owcą!" ("We'll make a sheep that isn't a sheep!") He needs a sheepskin, stuffing, a needle and thread, and "something that will give the dragon a terrible tummy ache."
- **Puzzle 1 – Dratewka's Lost Needle:** Dratewka can't sew without his big needle. It fell through a crack in the workshop floor.
  - Julek crawls under the workbench and comes back with it.
  - Gucio winds thread from a tangled spool. It's a simple untangling minigame where you follow one colour.
- **Act-end beat:** a deep rumble from the hill makes the tools rattle. Everyone freezes, and Julek hides behind Gucio. Dratewka whispers: "Smok się obudził. Musimy się pospieszyć." ("The dragon's awake. We have to hurry.")

### Act III: The Shepherd's Meadow (sheepskin and stuffing)

- The old shepherd's few remaining sheep are on the meadow by the river. He gives the boys an old sheepskin, but they need straw to stuff it.
- **Puzzle 2 – The Stubborn Ram:** the haystack is guarded by a grumpy ram who butts anyone who comes close.
  - Julek charms the ram. He offers it a dandelion and strokes its nose, and the ram goes all soft.
  - Gucio fills a sack with straw while the ram is busy.
  - Optional: glowing wool tufts lead to the dragon's footprints by the river, a quiet hint of where Kłębuszek is.
- **Act-end beat:** the shepherd, quietly: "Kłębuszek lubił tu skakać…" ("Kłębuszek liked to jump around here…") Gucio: "Przyprowadzimy go. Obiecuję." ("We'll bring him back. I promise.")

### Act IV: The Market and the Yellow Stones (sulphur)

- Dratewka explains: "Siarka! Śmierdzi okropnie. Smok od niej dostanie strasznego bólu brzucha." ("Sulphur! It stinks terribly. It will give the dragon a terrible tummy ache.") The merchant sells yellow, smelly stones.
- **Puzzle 3 – The Market Trade:** the merchant wants something in exchange. It's a short trade chain (2–3 steps) between the market stalls.
  - Julek's funny moment: he grabs a sulphur stone, sniffs it and makes his best "Fuj!" face, and Gucio takes it back.
- **Assembly:** back in the workshop, combine sheepskin + straw + sulphur, then needle and thread. It turns into a lumpy but convincing sheep. The townspeople who laughed now peek in through the window, curious, and Krak himself comes to look: "Szewcze… może to się uda." ("Shoemaker… maybe this will work.")

### Act V: The Dragon's Cave (the trick)

- At dusk, the cave at the foot of Wawel hill glows from the dragon's nostrils. The fake sheep goes onto a small cart.
- **Puzzle 4 – Fool the Dragon:**
  - The cart is stuck: a wheel came off. Gucio finds the wheel and a peg, and fixes it.
  - **The low point:** the dragon comes out, sniffs the sheep and isn't fooled: "Owca… która nie beczy? Hmmm…" ("A sheep… that doesn't bleat? Hmmm…") Silence, then music stops. Dratewka is shaking, and Gucio has no idea what to do.
  - Julek, hidden behind the sheep, remembers his bedtime game: "Beee!" The dragon is fooled. It's the payoff of the bedroom setup, and Julek's only noise trick in the game.
- **The dragon leaves (decided):** picture cards with sound. Gulp → tummy rumbles → he drinks the Vistula, and drinks, and drinks → round as a balloon → silence… → a gigantic **BEEEK!** that blows smoke rings over the river → he flies off over the mountains, holding his tummy, and never comes back.
- **The rescue (playable):** the boys go into the cave. It's full of sleepy sheep sitting on the dragon's piles of hay. Gucio opens the dragon's wooden gate, and Julek finds Kłębuszek last, curled up in a small hollow (a crawl, the emotional twin of the first puzzle).

### Ending: Kłębuszek Comes Home, the Birth of Kraków (cutscene, about 2 min)

Each character gets their own moment, and only one picture carries facts:
1. **The reunion:** the sheep stream out of the cave. Kłębuszek runs to the shepherd, who hugs him. Shepherd: "Dotrzymałeś słowa, chłopcze." ("You kept your word, boy.")
2. **Dratewka's moment:** the townspeople who laughed at him cheer him. Krak: "Szewczyku, byłeś mądrzejszy niż wszyscy moi rycerze." ("Little shoemaker, you were wiser than all my knights.") Dratewka points at the boys: "Sam bym nie dał rady." ("I couldn't have done it alone.")
3. **The feast and the name:** a feast on Wawel hill at sunset, with lanterns. The people shout the good king's name, and the town becomes Kraków. Gucio and Julek shout too, and Julek's version is "Kako!"
4. **The gift:** the shepherd gives Julek a soft tuft of wool from Kłębuszek, "dla najlepszego beczącego pomocnika" ("for the best bleating helper").
5. **The legend today (the one fact card):** the narrator, briefly: under Wawel, by the Vistula, a metal dragon still stands today, and it really breathes fire. "Ale owiec już nie zabiera." ("But it doesn't take sheep any more.")

### Back Home (bedroom, morning)

- Morning sun. Julek is sitting up, proudly holding a tuft of white wool. "Beee!"
- Mama comes in with the book: "To co, dokończymy legendę?" ("So, shall we finish the legend?") Gucio: "Mamo, ja wiem, jak się skończyła!" ("Mama, I know how it ended!") Julek: "Smok! Bum!" (he puffs out his cheeks for the burp).
- Mama laughs and looks at the wool, puzzled. "W wakacje pojedziemy do Krakowa. Zobaczycie prawdziwego smoka." ("In the holidays we'll go to Kraków. You'll see the real dragon.")
- Gucio, to the player: "To chyba nie był zwykły sen..." ("I don't think that was an ordinary dream...")

**THE END.**

## Design Guidelines & UI/UX

The series rules are unchanged:

- **Controls:** one tap does the obvious thing (take, talk, look). There are no verb icons. It works with a mouse and on touch screens.
- **Julek as a power (series mechanic):** Julek's face is the first, permanent inventory slot. When Gucio can't do something, he says who could.
- **No reading required:** full Polish voice-over and a picture-only inventory.
- **No failure state:** no game-overs, lives or timers. Wrong actions trigger funny lines.
- **Never scary:** the dragon is silly and greedy, never shown hurting anyone, and the sheep are always safe. The night prologue is cosy and moonlit, not dark. The cave is lit by the dragon's own glowing nostrils.
- **Hints:** glowing tufts of wool caught on fences and bushes show the way (where the sheep were carried off), and the boys remark on the next step.
- **Educational focus:** the legend of Krak and the Wawel Dragon, the name Kraków, Wawel hill and the Vistula, the real fire-breathing dragon statue, and cleverness and teamwork beating strength. The founding story is clearly framed as a legend.

### Cutscene craft

Within the engine's means (voiced picture cards with slow camera drift, characters walking and switching poses, objects fading in, music and sound):
- **Every cutscene has a feeling and a turn:** it starts in one mood and ends in another (calm → worry, fear → relief).
- **The narrator gets at most one line per card.** The rest is characters talking, and Gucio and Julek react in every cutscene.
- **Cards are built in layers** where it helps: a background plus a moving layer (the dragon's tail, smoke, sheep) that fades or drifts separately, so pictures feel alive.
- **Sound tells half the story:** each card has its own ambience, the music changes at turning points, and there's **silence before big moments** (the dragon's sniff, the BEEEK).
- **Short in-world beats between puzzles:** no big cutscenes, just 2–4 voiced lines with characters walking and posing, so the story keeps moving during play.
- **Every cutscene can be skipped** for replays.

## Scenes (approx.)

1. Bedroom (evening and morning). Reused from the series, made summery.
2. Dream prologue (picture cards).
3. The settlement street under Wawel.
4. Dratewka's workshop (interior).
5. The shepherd's meadow by the river.
6. The market.
7. The dragon's cave: outside at dusk, and inside for the rescue.
8. Ending (picture cards: the reunion, the cheering, the feast on the hill, the dragon statue today).

## Core Philosophy

A magical adventure first and a history lesson second. Gucio provides logic, curiosity and a promise he keeps, and Julek brings humour and the unexpected. The theme: **a clever idea and good friends beat a big sword.**
